The Foundation Cellar

Laetentur Caeli

Wines named for the people and moments that tried to make the Church breathe with both lungs

Why Wine

Let the Heavens Rejoice

Laetentur caeli are the first words of the decree of union read at the Council of Florence in 1439, when Greek and Latin bishops stood together and declared the Church one. The union did not last. The hope did.

The Likoudis family came from Kefalonia and settled in the Finger Lakes country of upstate New York, two places where the vine has been tended for generations. The wines come from those two places, and the Foundation’s work is the work of that council. These wines are a small way to bring both to the table. Each label tells one story of East and West, and every bottle supports the Foundation’s research, the Fellows Program, and The Kydones Review.

The First Release

A Red and a White

Red

Florence 1439

For the Council of Florence, where East and West signed one decree of union and read it aloud in Greek and Latin. A red for the long table, made to be opened with people you disagree with and love anyway.

From the Finger Lakes, likely a Cabernet Franc, or from Kefalonia, a Mavrodaphne.

White

Kydones

For Demetrios Kydones of Thessaloniki, the Byzantine statesman who translated Thomas Aquinas into Greek so that East and West could read each other. A bright white for the start of a long conversation.

From the Finger Lakes, likely a dry Riesling, or from Kefalonia, a Robola.

How It Works

Our Name, Their Cellar

The wines come from two places the family knows: the Finger Lakes of upstate New York and the island of Kefalonia. The Foundation does not make, sell, or ship wine. A licensed winery makes and bottles each wine under the Foundation’s labels, sells it from its own store, and ships it directly to you. The Foundation licenses its name and artwork to the winery, and the winery pays a royalty on every bottle back to the Foundation’s mission.

Purchases are not tax-deductible donations. They are a way to keep the conversation going at your own table.
