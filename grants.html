Research & Scholarship

Research & Publication Grants

Project funding for scholarship that advances Catholic–Orthodox understanding

About the Grants

Funding for Defined Projects

Alongside the residential Fellows Program, the Likoudis Legacy Foundation makes project grants to scholars, translators, and editors working on defined pieces of scholarship in the Foundation’s mission areas.

Where the Fellows Program supports a period of concentrated research on the work of James Likoudis, the grants program supports the project itself: a translation, a critical edition, an article or monograph, or the recovery of a neglected voice in the history of Catholic–Orthodox dialogue.

Grant amounts vary with available funding, and awards are announced each cycle. The Foundation’s role is often catalytic rather than total: a partial grant, a formal letter of support, an introduction to a publisher, or co-sponsorship can carry a strong project the rest of the way.

Priority Areas

What We Fund

Proposals are welcome in any area of the Foundation’s mission. Current priorities include:

  • —Catholic–Orthodox theology, history, and ecumenical dialogue
  • —Translations and critical editions of significant theological texts
  • —Recovery of neglected or untranslated voices in Christian thought
  • —Studies of the papacy, indefectibility, and the Byzantine schism
How Support Works

Two Forms of Support

$500–2,500

Project Micro-Grants

Direct awards for well-defined projects with a clear deliverable, made as funding allows. Suited to articles, single translations, editorial work, and archival research.

  • Awarded on a rolling basis each cycle
  • Paid as a lump sum on acceptance
  • Foundation acknowledged in resulting publications
Catalytic Support

Larger Projects

For book-length projects and multi-year editions, the Foundation contributes as one partner among several rather than as sole funder.

  • Partial grants toward defined project components
  • Formal letters of support for other funders
  • Publisher and network introductions
  • Co-sponsorship and joint fundraising where missions align

The Foundation does not publish a maximum award. Amounts above the micro-grant range depend on funding secured for each cycle; strong proposals that exceed current capacity are considered for catalytic support and kept on file as new funding is confirmed.

How to Apply

Grant Applications

Submit your proposal below. Supporting materials should be provided as links (Google Drive, Dropbox, or similar). Proposals are reviewed on a rolling basis, and every applicant receives a response.

A Complete Proposal Includes

  1. Project description and its contribution to the field
  2. Budget with the amount requested and other funding sources
  3. Timeline and publication plan
  4. CV or résumé for each principal
